For US and Canadian buyers, purchasing real estate in Mexico's "Restricted Zone" — which encompasses all property within 50 kilometers of the coastline, including Puerto Vallarta — is made completely secure through a bank trust known as a **Fideicomiso**.

While foreign investors understand that a Fideicomiso grants them full ownership rights to use, sell, or pass down property, confusion often arises when transitioning from personal owner use to operating a revenue-generating rental asset.

*Can you legally rent a property held in a Fideicomiso? Who handles the operational burden when you live thousands of miles away? How do you ensure legal and financial compliance?*

Below is a complete operational and legal breakdown for foreign owners managing their Vallarta real estate remotely in 2027.

## 1. Can You Rent a Property Held Under a Fideicomiso?

Yes, absolutely. Under Mexican law, the beneficiary of a Fideicomiso (*el fideicomisario*) holds the exclusive right to use, enjoy, lease, and profit from the real estate asset.

The bank acting as your trustee (*el fiduciario*) holds title to the property for legal compliance, but **they do not participate in or claim any right to your rental income**.

However, operating a rental remotely introduces two distinct responsibilities:

1. **Legal & tax obligations:** Registering for local tax compliance (SAT) to declare rental earnings derived within Mexican territory.
2. **Operational obligations:** Managing guest check-ins, emergency maintenance, and platform listings directly on-site.

## 2. Structure of Remote Property Management Under a Trust

### Key Roles in Remote Asset Care

- **The trustee bank:** Maintains title compliance and collects annual trust maintenance fees.
- **The owner (you):** Retains 100% equity, receives net profits, and decides operational parameters.
- **The local property manager:** Acts as your physical representative in Puerto Vallarta, overseeing physical care, guest experiences, and maintenance.

## 4. Key Clauses Every Foreign Property Owner Needs in Their Management Agreement

When hiring a local property management company in Puerto Vallarta, ensure your service agreement protects your asset held in trust:

1. **Non-exclusive operational authority:** The agency operates on your behalf but holds zero equity claim over your real estate asset or Fideicomiso rights.
2. **Transparent fee structure:** Avoid contracts with hidden markups on routine repairs, emergency calls, or guest check-in services.
3. **Audit-ready reporting:** Ensure monthly statements itemize all gross bookings, digital platform withholdings, and verifiable expense receipts (CFDIs).
